Challenge Prompt
Ghost in the Machine
Build something with open-source AI at its core.
That can mean running an open-weight model, building on an open-source agent harness or framework, running inference locally, or all three.  Whatever you pick, the open pieces should be what makes your project work.
In your post, tell us
why open innovation matters for what you built
.  Does it run on a laptop with no internet?  Keep someone's data off a server they don't control?  Let you fine-tune, swap models, or change how your agent behaves?  Cost nothing to run?  Tell us where your open-based approach worked better than a closed one.
This Week's Theme: Ghost in the Machine
Build something spooky, or something that honors the past in the spirit of Día de los Muertos, with open-weight models or open-source AI.
This week brings us Halloween on October 31 and Día de los Muertos on November 1 and 2, a stretch of the calendar when the line between past and present feels a little thinner.  Whether that brings to mind things that go bump in the night, ofrendas glowing with candlelight and marigolds, or the memories and stories we carry with us, we want to see what it inspires you to build.  The theme is intentionally broad, so interpret it however you'd like!
That can mean running an open-weight model, building on an open-source agent harness or framework, running inference locally, or all three.  Whatever you pick, the open pieces should be what makes your project work.
A few ideas to get you going:
A haunted terminal that "possesses" your shell and whispers back eerie commentary on the commands you run
A virtual ofrenda builder that helps you write a remembrance for a loved one and gather their favorite foods, songs, and stories
An offline séance chatbot that channels the spirit of a long-dead programming language
A local oral history tool that records and transcribes family stories so they're never lost
A ghost story generator that weaves your smart home's sensor logs into a creepy tale of what happened while you were asleep
A project that brings an abandoned open-source tool or a forgotten corner of internet history back to life
In your post, tell us
why open innovation matters for what you built
.  Does running your own model mean your project works with the lights (and the internet) off?  Keep your family's photos and stories private and on your own machine?  Make sure what you build isn't locked inside a service that might disappear someday?  Tell us where your open-based approach worked better than a closed one.
